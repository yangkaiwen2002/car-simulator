ADAPTATIONS IN OPENROAD
Original JOE v3 browser loader; winding converted to counterclockwise;
texture upload orientation adjusted; paint alpha interpreted as paint mask;
rim dimensions adapted to the .car tire specification; tire geometry generated;
transparent glass material and browser camera positions adapted.
Engine sample playback rate varies with simulated RPM. Samples are community
assets, not verified recordings of each factory vehicle.
The shared source steering-wheel model is not a separate factory wheel per car.
Original urban environment, circuit scenery and website interface.
UI hierarchy references the vehicle showcase of Forza Horizon and compact
driving telemetry of iRacing; no logos, screenshots, fonts or UI assets copied.
Functional gauge faces added to TC6 and MI blank dashboard areas, including
speed, RPM, gear and on MI session time / throttle / brake. This is a simulation
adaptation, not a factory instrument replica. MI steering position adjusted
to avoid intersection with its dashboard.

PHYSICS AND REFERENCE PROJECTS
VDrift: https://github.com/VDrift/vdrift
JOE format and .car parameter semantics were studied to load the original assets.
Sketchbook: https://github.com/swift502/Sketchbook
Car.ts was studied for browser control patterns, steering smoothing, gear
transitions and first-person interaction. Its implementation is not bundled.
The OpenRoad physics solver is original, not the full solver from either project.
It uses a dynamic two-axle model: axle slip angles, nonlinear lateral forces,
combined longitudinal/lateral grip, load transfer, yaw inertia, torque interpolation,
automatic gears, aerodynamic drag/downforce, rolling resistance and graded roads.
Default assists limit wheel lock at speed, countersteer through tire angles, and
reduce drive torque to available grip. No path-following or automatic corner braking.
Service brakes use an idealized ABS limit. Pedal force is progressive; braking cuts
throttle, including W+S. Grip coefficients and aero are game calibrations.
No full 3D suspension, wheel rotational inertia/temperature model, drivetrain elasticity,
manufacturer validation, traffic AI, rollover or soft-body deformation is included.
An original planar rigid-body contact solver uses SAT manifolds, normal and
friction impulses, angular momentum, broad-phase hashing and adaptive substeps.
Damage/visual crumpling and extreme-impact explosion are game approximations,
not manufacturer crash simulation or predictions of real accident outcomes.
All displayed performance figures are community configuration values.

Track geometry © OpenTrackData, derived from © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL).
Elevation: AWS Terrain Tiles. Source: https://github.com/chendo/opentrackdata
Original path: data/tracks/nordschleife.geojson. The source commit is embedded in
circuits/nordschleife.geojson. This openly downloadable derived GeoJSON is ODbL 1.0;
see circuits/NORDSCHLEIFE-LICENSE.txt and NORDSCHLEIFE-ATTRIBUTION.txt.
Adaptation: extracted the ordered lap_profile; removed consecutive duplicates;
projected horizontal coordinates without rescaling. Terrain elevations are smoothed
at runtime over a 40 m radius for drivable grades, not laser-scanned road heights.
The projected centerline is about 20.752 km; UI shows the official nominal 20.832 km.
https://www.nuerburgring.de/info/nuerburgring/race-tracks/nordschleife
The challenge line is the dataset origin near Hatzenbach, NOT the official T13 line.
Width (9 m), scenery, guardrails and equal-third splits are game approximations.
No surveyed bank angle, Karussell banking, suspension motion or airborne dynamics.
